# Dashboard design notes

## Layout

The desk opens with a small welcome message and a shop overview row for open-ticket count, the five-agent team, and the live checking balance. Below that, the screen is split into a ticket rail and a larger work area. The rail keeps all three tickets visible and selectable; the work area focuses on the selected ticket, its verified request details, the agent team, run notes, Boss summary, and any human decision waiting for review. The layout narrows into stacked sections on smaller screens.

This keeps the main flow easy to scan: choose one ticket, understand its facts, start a focused run, follow the work, then make any final decision. The "one ticket at a time" language and small flower motif make a busy operational board feel more like a considerate shop workroom than an alarm-heavy control panel.

## How the agents read differently

Each agent has a compact, consistently placed card with its name, a distinct icon/color, and a plain-language state: **On standby**, **At work**, **Done**, or **Needs attention**. Boss uses a heart mark, Inventory a stock/inbox mark, Accounting a payment mark, Facilities a sparkle mark, and Customer Service a message mark. Their identity colors (pink, lilac, mint, warm cream, and blue-lilac) help people find a role quickly without making the cards feel like competing alerts.

The card copy changes as audit events arrive, so it can show what that agent is investigating rather than a generic permanent description. A small tool label and database-check count make tool use visible at a glance. The run-notes feed supplies the sequence behind those summaries, with expandable database results and verified facts where available. This pairing gives a quick overview for scanning and a deeper trail when a person wants evidence.

## Resolved tickets and cash

Every ticket card and the selected-ticket heading show a status pill. Open tickets use a warm amber treatment; resolved tickets use a calm mint/green treatment, so status is conveyed by both text and color. Resolution remains an explicit human action after the agent report is reviewed; finishing a run alone does not resolve a ticket.

Checking appears in the overview as a prominent, currency-formatted balance, with the shop date alongside it. The dashboard refreshes the figure from the backend, including after an approved payment or purchase outflow, so a cash change is visible without implying that money came in. Mint accents distinguish the balance from the open-ticket count, while approval controls remain separate and require deliberate human confirmation.

## Why these choices work for people

The dark charcoal and plum surfaces with soft pink highlights feel warm and cozy while maintaining strong contrast and a clear visual hierarchy. Rounded cards, generous spacing, short labels, and familiar icons make the page approachable without hiding important operational details. Gentle status language and the quiet empty state reduce pressure when nothing is happening; a live indicator and timestamped notes add urgency only while work is actually underway. Most importantly, recommendations, evidence, cash, and human approval are visually distinct, reinforcing that agents can investigate and advise while people retain control over consequential actions.
